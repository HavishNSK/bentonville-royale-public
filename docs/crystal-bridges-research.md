# Crystal Bridges Museum: Architecture Research for a Stylized Rebuild

Research notes for modeling the real Crystal Bridges Museum of American Art (Moshe Safdie, opened Nov 11 2011; Safdie expansion opened June 6 2026) in the game's `crystal_bridges` tile. This file records facts and labels inferences. It is not a build spec.

Labels: **[F]** = stated by a source I opened (named). **[INF]** = my inference from plans, OSM, or terrain. **[OSM]** = measured from repo OSM data.

Compiled 2026-11-17. All external content was treated as untrusted reference data.

---

## 0. Key takeaways

- **Ravine and ponds [F, Safdie site, audio script].** The ravine runs north-south. Town Branch Creek, fed by Crystal Spring, flows **north**. Two bridge pavilions act as weirs and create two ponds:
  - **South pond** = "upper pond" (the audio script's name).
  - **North pond** = "lower pond".
  - The upper pond drops **10 ft** over the weir under the Eleven bridge (audio script). Safdie and McGill-search text say **12 ft**.
- **The two bridge buildings sit at opposite ends of the north pond [F, Safdie site, Acentech via search].**
  - North end: the **Modern Art gallery bridge**.
  - South end: the **Eleven restaurant bridge**.
- **Great Hall [F, Safdie site].** It sits on the south pond and is surrounded by water on three sides, like a peninsula (Archello via search). The curved, glass-walled **Great Hall Corridor** links it along the **east** shore to the main lobby.
- **Entry [F, audio script, Safdie].** You enter at the **east crest** (Museum Way drop-off, upper parking) through a curved colonnade, then an elevator tower down into the ravine. The main lobby (Garrison Lobby) is at the **east end of the Eleven bridge**.
- **2026 expansion [F, Arch Record, Dezeen].** About 114,000 sq ft, built **north (downstream)** of the original. A U-shaped set of buildings plus a Bridge Gallery with a café makes the complex a "complete figure-eight loop". It adds a **North Entrance**.

## 2. Spatial arrangement

The orientation comes from the Inside Guide floor plan (no compass on it) plus OSM. Five checks all agree that the plan is **north-up**:

- The plan's long axis is vertical, like the OSM footprint (332 m N-S × 121 m E-W).
- The Main Entrance and Upper Parking arrows point **east**, toward Museum Way (OSM x ≈ +60).
- The South Entrance and Art Trail point toward downtown to the **south** (OSM Art Trail at z ≈ −135…−160).
- The creek flows north (the script says it returns "to the natural stream bed to the north").
- The OSM Eleven POI and the 100-ft bridge fall where the plan puts them.

### Visitor walk order (audio script)

1. Main (Garrison) Lobby, then the double doors opposite Guest Services.
2. **Early American gallery** (compressed entry, curved walls, glulam beams).
3. Glass reflection hallway at the **East Terrace**, looking across the north pond to Contemporary.
4. Next gallery ("People on the Move"; red-oak floors).
5. "Notions of Beauty" (door view down to **Walker Landing**).
6. **Reflection Room** with the bridge's cable abutment and a two-storey window wall.
7. Small gallery, then stairs down (Gabriel Dawe installation overhead) to the **Lower North Gallery** (compression). The north elevator tower doors are here.
8. **Modern Art gallery bridge**: lawn view north, pond view south. This is "Safdie's favorite view": entrance, current position, and destination all visible.
9. Down the glass walls and a hallway to the **Contemporary (1940s to Now)** gallery on the west, one level lower. Balcony at its midpoint.
10. Up stairs or elevator in the final reflection space to the **Eleven bridge** (lower pond behind you, upper pond and Great Hall ahead to the south).
11. **Coffee Bar**, back to the Lobby (Museum Store across the courtyard).
12. Turn right down the **Great Hall Corridor** (library on the 3rd floor above, balcony, tulip-tree cut-out, Corridor Reflection Room).
13. **South Lobby**, then out to the Art Trail and the FLW house.

The loop runs counter-clockwise around the north pond (east, then north, then west, then south over Eleven), then down the east shore of the south pond [INF from plan].

## 3. Materials and details by room

From the audio script unless another source is named.

- **Whole building:**
  - Cast-in-place architectural concrete walls 12–18 in thick (24 in below ground).
  - About 45,000 cu yd of concrete in total.
  - Every concrete surface is hand-polished to a marble-like finish.
  - Outer walls carry inset **cedar heartwood bands**, about 20,000 linear ft. Exposed bands have faded silver; protected bands keep a red-gold color. The bands cover the form-tie divots and line up with window mullions.
  - Copper roofs were shiny orange when new and have oxidized to **deep brown**, with blue-green streaks only where runoff reaches the concrete.
  - Other sources: "Western red cedar" (Arch Record, Wikipedia); fieldstone (Wikipedia, Safdie site); "wood lattice space-frames" and "laminated timber arch roof structures" for the bridges (Safdie site).
- **Module rhythm:** the same bay spacing repeats in the entry colonnade, the lobby columns, the gallery beams, and the bridge glass panels. Each bay is about two paces [F]. A 3–4 m module is [INF].
- **Early American galleries:**
  - Narrow entry that opens out.
  - Curved long walls following the ravine.
  - Asymmetric curved ceiling: high on the ravine side, low on the pond side.
  - Exposed structural beams ("honesty of materials").
- **"People on the Move" gallery:**
  - Glulam beams of **Southern white pine**, made in Magnolia, AR.
  - **Red oak floors**, milled in Monticello, AR.
  - Benches from oak and walnut cut on site.
- **"Notions of Beauty" gallery:** roof beams pass through the glass to the outside.
- **Reflection Room:** cannon-like end of a 4-inch suspension cable set into the concrete abutment. Abutment form-tie divots are left visible. Cedar-banded side walls. Two-storey window wall.
- **Lower North Gallery:** low ceiling and tight walls ("compression").
- **Modern Art bridge:**
  - Expansive "in-between space" with skylights.
  - Concrete abutment arch overhead.
  - Glass walls angled out about 12° on vertical poles with "spider connectors".
  - Accordion joints at the ceiling let the suspended roof move.
  - Each roof beam bolts around the suspension cables at both ends.
- **Contemporary gallery:**
  - Higher ceilings than the Early American galleries.
  - Long curved wall.
  - Glass vestibule leading to a small pond balcony.
- **Eleven:**
  - Skylights and curved roof beams ("ribcage").
  - Floor of **green quartzite** tile from China, chosen to match the deep-green pond color. The pond turns muddy brown after rain.
- **Coffee Bar and Museum Store:** interiors by Marlon Blackwell. The store has cherrywood ribs and a living green roof (CB architecture page).
- **Great Hall Corridor:**
  - Light-filled colonnade where water reflections ripple on the walls and ceiling.
  - Polished concrete columns.
  - Balcony with a view of the Great Hall roof cables anchored to bedrock.
  - Semicircular cut-out in the back curve for the tulip trees.
- **Corridor Reflection Room:** large windows onto a native-plant rock garden, which is also a dry-creek drainage channel.
- **Walker Landing:** concrete circular amphitheater tiers stepping down into the water.
- **Weirs:** zig-zag "labyrinth weirs" under the Eleven bridge. The creek carries about 500,000 gal/day.
- **Bridge pavilions:** floors and glass rest on concrete piers. Only the roof hangs from the cables.

Roof shape, inferred from the descriptions [INF]:
- **Bridges (Eleven, Modern Art):** convex domed copper roofs that "rise like mounds of earth across the still ponds" (CB architecture page), on cables between abutments.
- **Hillside galleries:** single-curvature roofs curving inward (concave soffit), sloping from the high ravine edge down to the pond.

## 4. The 2026 expansion

Sources: Architectural Record and Dezeen were opened. The museum's own pages and press were seen only in search results unless stated.

- **Timeline:** groundbreaking 2022. Public opening **June 6–7, 2026**.
- **Size:** about **114,000 sq ft**. Sources disagree on growth: about 50% (museum, Dezeen) versus doubling (Arch Record, Artnet).
- **Location:** a caption puts the original museum "to the south" of the expansion, "located just downstream" (Arch Record). So the expansion is **north** of the Modern Art bridge, in the downstream ravine.
- **Form (Dezeen):**
  - A **U-shaped** volume at the bottom of the site.
  - With the original, it forms "a complete figure-eight loop" around a rerouted stream.
  - A **Glass Bridge Gallery** spans the pond and links the two new galleries. Search summaries mention a 15,000 sq ft pond. It holds the **40-seat café Quartz + Honey** at its midpoint.
  - A second entrance at the **northern end** (the "North Entrance").
  - A **circular outdoor events plaza** "floats above a pond".
- **New spaces:**
  - Contemporary American Art Gallery.
  - **North Temporary Exhibition Gallery**, about 14,000 sq ft, with north-facing reflector skylights. It sits "to the east", one level above the educational spaces.
  - Creative-learning **Hub**, about 14,000 sq ft: studios, classrooms, ceramics and digital studios. It has a **sawtooth roof**.
- **Materials:**
  - Long-span **Southern yellow pine glulam** (Alabama-milled per Arch Record; Dezeen says local NW Arkansas forests).
  - Barrel-vaulted glulam ceiling in the bridge gallery.
  - Roofs are **steel standing-seam** (Dezeen), not copper.
  - Safdie: "stay within the vocabulary of the existing museum, with one exception" (the Hub / North Temporary Gallery palette).
- **Landscape:** about 5 acres by Coen+Partners.

How the expansion maps onto OSM [INF]:
- The museum relation's northern part (game z −360…−474) has no counterpart in the 2024 venue map or the 2023 Inside Guide.
- Its OSM ways have recent IDs (1531538948/949/950).
- I therefore read everything north of the Modern Art bridge as the expansion:
  - **West wing:** x −55…−33, z −364…−454.
  - **East wing:** x 33…60, z −350…−454 (likely the North Temporary Gallery with the Hub below, since sources say "to the east").
  - **Bridge Gallery band** across the north end: z ≈ −437…−454, x −24…43.
  - **North Entrance projection:** x −39…−23, z −450…−474.
  - **New pond** (open courtyard): z −359…−440.
  - **Round plaza:** the circular notch in the water ring at about (2, −405), r ≈ 9–11 m, beside a mapped splash pad.

### Conflicts and caveats

- **Weir drop:** 10 ft (audio script) vs 12 ft (Safdie site). Terrain suggests about 12–15 ft [INF].
- **Expansion size:** "+50%" vs "doubles the footprint".
- **Expansion glulam origin:** Alabama-milled (Arch Record) vs local NW Arkansas forests (Dezeen).
- **Plans are schematic:** the Inside Guide (2023) and Venue map (2024) predate the 2026 expansion and are not to scale. The OSM outline is crowd-mapped. Treat the pavilion-to-polygon assignments in section 5 as best-fit inferences.
- **Fly's Eye Dome:** 50 ft diameter (Outside Guide: 38 × 50 × 50 ft). It is on the North Lawn / Orchard Trail; no position is mapped in OSM.
- **North Lawn** (Venue map): 180 × 755 ft.
